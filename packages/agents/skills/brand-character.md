## Principles
A brand is a character, and the kit is evidence of it. Before you plan, write or design, read every brand input together: the kit's colours, type and marks, the brand sheet pages, the site and product pictures, the Feel and Look answers, the voice rules, the facts. Then settle, for yourself, five things.

1. Temperament. How this brand behaves in a room: loud or quiet, playful or exact, warm or cool, fast or unhurried. Take it from how its own materials behave, never from its category.
2. Signature. The two or three things that make its materials recognisable with the logo covered: a colour and how much of the surface it takes, a type voice, a graphic gesture, a way of cropping or framing, a material, a kind of light.
3. Proportions. Which colour is the ground, which is the voice and which is the accent, in roughly what shares. A brand whose materials live on light grounds does not become dark because dark looks premium, and the reverse. An accent stays an accent.
4. Speech. How it talks: sentence length, humour, directness, what it would never say.
5. Substance. What only this brand has to show: its product in use, its interface, its outputs, its people, its numbers.

Every decision afterwards must be explainable from these five. The test for any frame, line or idea: with the logo covered, would someone who knows the brand name it? If the frame would fit another brand after swapping the logo and one colour, it is not this brand yet. Rules in the kit are limits you may not cross; character is what you build with inside them. When the materials disagree, the brand's own finished work outranks its written rules, and the written rules outrank your taste.

## Checks
- [ideas] Does the idea use something only this brand has: its product, interface, outputs, people or numbers?
- Can someone who knows the brand name it with the logo covered?
- Are the brand's ground, voice and accent colours used in the brand's own proportions?
- Is the accent colour limited to a small share of the frame?

## Signs for the reviewer
The frame would fit another brand after swapping the logo and one colour.
The ground is dark where the brand's own materials are light, or the reverse.
The accent colour covers the frame instead of marking one point.
