## Principles
A layout is finished when it looks intended at every ordered size, not when it looks right at one.

Derive everything from the frame. Take one unit from the shorter side of the frame and express margins, gaps, radii, stroke widths and type sizes as multiples of it. The same design then scales as one object.

Use a small scale of spaces (a tight one inside a group, a medium one between groups, a large one around the whole). Things that belong together sit closer to each other than to anything else; equal gaps everywhere erase the structure.

Align to few lines. Pick the edges and axes the frame will use and put every element on one of them. An element that is nearly aligned reads as a mistake; either align it or move it clearly away.

Respect safe areas and platform overlays: keep words and the mark inside the safe area the format gives, and keep the lower part of tall formats free of anything that must be read.

Pictures fill their area by cover with a chosen focal point, so the subject survives every crop. Decide per format what may be cropped and what never is. Never stretch a picture or a mark.

Check edges: nothing touches the frame edge by accident, nothing overlaps unless it is meant to, no text sits closer to an edge than the inner margin. Optical corrections are allowed: large type and round shapes need slightly less margin to look aligned.

Measure after rendering. If a measured finding says a word is clipped, overlapping or too small, change the rule that produced it, so the fix holds for every variation and not only for the view you looked at.

## Checks
- Are margins, gaps and type sizes visibly multiples of one unit in every format?
- Does every element sit on one of a few shared edges or axes?
- Are every word and the mark inside the safe area in each format?
- Is the subject of every picture still visible after each format's crop?

## Signs for the reviewer
An element is almost aligned with another.
Equal gaps everywhere hide which things belong together.
Something touches a frame edge by accident.
