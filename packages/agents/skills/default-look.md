## Signs for the reviewer
When nothing in a frame was decided from the brand and the idea, the result drifts to a recognisable default. Treat each of these as a sign the decision was skipped, and replace it with a choice made from the brand's character and the promise.

- A dark or grey empty space with a soft spotlight, standing in for a subject.
- Glowing, glassy or softly shadowed rounded panels arranged in a row or a grid.
- A plinth, podium, shelf or stage holding an abstract or generic object.
- A smooth generic object whose only link to the product is a figure of speech.
- Gradients, blurs and glows used to make flat shapes look finished.
- A headline placed in a box because nothing else gave it a place.
- Everything centred, evenly spaced and the same size.
- The brand's accent colour used as a thin line or edge on an otherwise brandless frame.
- An interface drawn as empty cards with no believable content.
- A picture that shows the category in general and nothing of this product.
- Colours, materials or moods taken from what seems premium instead of from the brand's own materials.

Finding one of these is not a matter of taste: it means the frame says nothing about this product for this viewer. Go back to the promise and the proof and ask what should be standing there. Usually the answer is the product working, a person in the named moment, or the headline itself at full scale in the brand's voice. Empty space is allowed only when it makes the one dominant element stronger.
