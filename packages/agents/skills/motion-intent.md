## Principles
Motion in an ad has one job: to deliver the promise in the order the viewer should understand it. Plan it from the idea, and make sure the still frame already works without it.

The decisive frame first. Each scene has one moment where the idea is complete; design that as the key frame. Motion is the way to arrive there and to leave, not a substitute for a frame that says nothing.

The first second must hold on its own: the subject or the first words are on screen at once. Never start on an empty or nearly empty frame.

One movement at a time carries the meaning. Decide what moves, from where to where, and why this serves the promise (reveals the product, shows the change, counts the benefit, points at the offer). Everything else stays still or follows quietly.

Order of appearance is the reading order: what must be understood first appears first; the offer and the call to action arrive last and stay long enough to read twice.

Character comes from the brand. A precise brand moves in short, exact steps; a warm one eases; a playful one overshoots a little. Use one timing character throughout a film.

Describe motion concretely for the next stage: what element, what property (position, scale, opacity, mask, colour), start and end state, duration in seconds, and the feel of the easing. A viewer with sound off and attention elsewhere should still get the promise from the frames at one, three and the last second.

## Checks
- Does each scene's key frame show its subject and its words without any motion?
- Is the subject or the first line visible in the first frame of the first scene?
- Do the offer and the call to action appear in the last scene?

## Signs for the reviewer
The first scene opens on an empty or nearly empty frame.
A key frame says nothing without the motion planned for it.
