## Principles
A frame in a feed has under a second. It stops a viewer only when one thing dominates and that thing is concrete.

One dominant element. Decide what the eye lands on first and give it far more area, contrast or colour than anything else: a face, a hand doing something, the product at a striking scale, a single huge word, one saturated shape on a quiet ground. Two elements of similar weight cancel each other.

Concrete beats abstract. People, hands, real objects, the product in use, a result you can read. Empty rooms, floating panels, glows, generic shapes and soft gradients are background at best; they are never the subject.

Contrast at thumbnail size. Shrink the frame in your mind to a few centimetres: the dominant element and the headline must still separate from the ground. If the frame turns into a grey average, raise the contrast of value first, then of colour, then of scale.

Fill the frame. The subject and the headline together should own most of the surface. Small type in a large calm field reads as unfinished, not as elegant. Margins are deliberate, never leftover.

Energy. Diagonals, cropping that cuts into the subject, a gesture caught mid-action, an element that breaks a boundary, light with a direction. A frame where everything is centred, level and evenly spaced is calm; make it calm only when calm is the idea.
