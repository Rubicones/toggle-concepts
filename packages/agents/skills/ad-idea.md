## Principles
An ad earns attention by promising the viewer something they want and showing it is true. Work in this order.

The promise. One benefit, in the viewer's words, about their situation; a description of the company is not a promise. If the line could be the first slide of a company presentation, it is not a promise yet.

The tension. What the viewer is stuck with today, shown as a recognisable moment of their own work or life. Name the moment exactly: who, where, doing what, annoyed by what.

The proof. The product, or its result, visibly doing what the promise says, with believable specifics from confirmed facts. A metaphor may frame the proof; an abstract object standing where the product should be proves nothing and reads as decoration. If the viewer cannot tell what is being sold from the picture and the headline together, the idea has failed, however elegant it looks.

The turn. Something in the frame the viewer did not expect: an exaggeration of the benefit, a contrast between before and after, a literal reading of a figure of speech, a familiar thing behaving differently, a surprising scale. One turn, carried by the picture and the headline together; the headline should need the picture and the picture the headline.

Boldness is a commitment to one of these moves at full strength, with everything else removed. A careful average of safe choices is the most common failure, and it is invisible in a feed. Choose the version a cautious committee would soften, as long as it stays true to the facts and to the brand's character.
